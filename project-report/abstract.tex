{\singlespacing
Positional and tracking data are central to modern football analytics, but
remain largely confined to elite settings through proprietary commercial
systems. Broadcast video offers a more accessible and reproducible source
of tactical information, yet open pipelines commonly stop at intermediate
outputs such as detections, tracks, or top-down visualisations rather than
the event-level analysis that motivates tracking data.

This project develops and evaluates a modular computer vision pipeline
that converts broadcast football video into tactical outputs: bird's-eye
visualisations, heatmaps, possession assignments, pass and offside event
logs, and per-player match statistics. The system combines a YOLOv8
four-class detector with a dedicated ball detector, ByteTrack multi-object
tracking, unsupervised SigLIP-UMAP-$k$-Means team classification, and a
YOLOv8-pose pitch-keypoint detector feeding a stride-cached Direct Linear Transform (DLT) homography
estimator. All downstream reasoning is performed in pitch-space
coordinates.

All six pre-registered success criteria are met within the defined evaluation setting. The generic detector
achieves test $\mathrm{mAP}_{50}=0.855$, while the dedicated ball detector
improves ball-class $\mathrm{mAP}_{50}$ from $0.459$ to $0.950$.
ByteTrack attains $\mathrm{HOTA}=0.668$ on a combined
$3{,}250$-frame evaluation set and outperforms BoT-SORT on HOTA, IDF1,
and identity-switch count. Team classification reaches mean macro-$F_1 =
0.993$ across three independent broadcast clips. At tactical level, pass
detection achieves $F_1=0.903$, all ten flagged offside-position events were manually verified as correct, 
and pitch-space reprojection error remains within the tactical-scale tolerance, 
with a median error of $169.9$\,cm and a mean error of $199.4$\,cm.

The main contribution is an end-to-end, reproducible broadcast-analysis
pipeline that requires no per-match manual labelling at inference time and produces quantitatively evaluated and visually inspectable tactical outputs. 
The results show that broadcast-only football
analysis can move beyond detection and tracking towards interpretable
tactical summaries, while identifying key limitations in homography
accuracy, possession transitions, and speed estimation.

}
